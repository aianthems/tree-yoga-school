# Tree Yoga School

**Rooted in nature. Practiced in the real world. Built in public.**

Tree Yoga School is a yoga school founded by **Alex Julian**, now being renewed as a living, AI-native public project. Its foundation is the original *Tree Yoga School* book: a body of teaching that brings together yoga, meditation, time among trees, and the practice of learning from nature.

> Nature is the teacher. Technology is the tool. Practice is the point.

[Read the original book][book] · [Explore the curriculum](#the-original-curriculum) · [Build with us](#contributing)

**Project status:** Foundation stage. The book exists; the digital school is being built. This repository does not yet contain a runnable website or an AI teaching assistant.

## What is Tree Yoga?

Tree Yoga is not simply Tree Pose performed outdoors. It is a practice of bringing attention, movement, breath, and contemplation into a relationship with actual, living trees.

The book introduces **three ways to practice**:

**Yoga with a tree.** Explore postures and movement beside a tree, using the trunk for appropriate support where the person, tree, and setting allow it. Stillness and simple movements belong here just as much as challenging poses.

**Meditation with a tree.** Sit, stand, or rest nearby. Observe the breath, notice the surroundings, and return to the present moment. The book includes chair-based, seated, reclined, and standing approaches.

**Tree Yoga Hiking.** Bring the practice into a nature walk. Rather than treating every tree as scenery on the way to a destination, pause, observe, and become acquainted with the place you are moving through.

These pathways come from [“Who and How,” chapter 3][chapter-3]. Underneath all three is the same invitation: **stop passing nature by, and begin spending time with it.**

## Begin with a tree—not an account

You do not need to learn GitHub, use AI, or wait for a website to begin.

As a simple introduction, choose a safe, permitted, accessible place near a tree. Find a comfortable position. Put the screen aside, notice your surroundings, and let your breathing remain comfortable. Observe the bark, the canopy, the changing light, or the sounds around you. There is nothing particular you need to feel or achieve.

The book recommends beginning a new habit with five or ten minutes rather than an all-or-nothing commitment ([chapter 4, printed p. 28][small-beginnings]). Start smaller when needed. Quiet observation is a complete first visit; physical contact, special equipment, and advanced postures are not prerequisites.

This introductory invitation is an adaptation for the public project, not a verbatim exercise from the book. Read [Care and safety](#care-and-safety) before exploring movement practices.

## The original curriculum

The source is **Alex Julian’s *Tree Yoga School* (2023)**, ISBN **9798752094705**. The book traces the school’s development through yoga practice and teaching, forest bathing, mentorship, and engagement with the *Bhagavad Gita*, *Tao Te Ching*, and *Yoga Sutras of Patanjali* ([chapter 1][chapter-1]).

This repository is a new home for that work—not a claim that AI invented it. The book remains the reference for what the original school taught. New lessons, revisions, and interpretations should be clearly identified as such.

## A philosophy we can practice—and build by

Trees are not merely a backdrop for this school. In the book, they become subjects of attention and sources of reflection on how to live.

The ten principles in *Wisdom and Wonder* provide a foundation for the new project. The applications below are **new interpretations for this public edition**, not quotations from the book.

| Principle from the book | How we can put it into practice here |
| --- | --- |
| Acceptance | Begin with the body, understanding, and resources available today. |
| Adaptability | Offer different ways to participate instead of demanding one ideal form. |
| Balance and Optimization | Use technology deliberately; leave time and attention for life away from it. |
| One-Pointed Focus and Aspiration for Excellence | Give one practice—or one useful improvement—your full attention. |

| Presence | Notice what is actually happening, rather than performing for an imagined audience. |
| Release | Let go of unnecessary features, rigid expectations, and work that no longer serves the school. |

| Unconditional Forgiveness | Make room for mistakes and repair while respecting healthy boundaries. |
| Unconditional Love | Welcome people with care, and extend that care to trees and their surroundings. |

## What AI-native means here

**AI should help us build a better school—not become the object of the practice.**

The intention is to use AI thoughtfully in organizing material, improving explanations, developing software, and exploring new teaching formats. The original work supplies the roots; people remain responsible for what is published.

Our design commitments are:

- **Keep the sources visible.** Connect adapted lessons to the relevant book pages. Distinguish original text, editorial changes, personal reflections, and research-backed claims.
- **Keep human judgment central.** Review AI-assisted contributions for accuracy, safety, attribution, and tone. An AI response is not a medical assessment or a substitute for qualified instruction.
- **Make the school understandable and accessible.** Favor readable content, clear navigation, and tools that support different ways of learning. Future features belong on the roadmap until they work.
- **Help people return to practice.** Build useful stopping points, not reasons to scroll forever. A successful session may end with closing the device and going outside.

**AI-native is a direction for development, not a claim that autonomous teaching features are already available.**

## A digital practice

The book’s final chapter encourages journaling, making nature a habit, and contributing insights. Building in public can be one more expression of that invitation:

**Practice → Notice → Reflect → Make one useful change → Return outside.**

A useful change might be a clearer explanation, a corrected reference, an accessibility improvement, or a carefully considered question. Small contributions count. Not every session needs a commit, and not every meaningful experience needs to become public content.

The measure is not a contribution streak. It is whether the work helps someone practice with greater attention, care, and independence.

## What exists, and what comes next

The repository currently contains `README.md`, `.gitignore`, and `LICENSE`. The original book is linked above rather than copied into the repository. There is no application, dependency manifest, database, or installation command yet.

The intended sequence is deliberately small:

| Stage | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| **Foundation — now** | Establish the school’s identity, source material, scope, and contribution approach. |
| **First lessons — next** | Develop a small, reviewed collection of introductory practices, with source references and clear adaptations. |
| **Minimal website — planned** | Present the material in a calm, accessible website. Next.js with Markdown/MDX content and Vercel hosting is the proposed direction, not an implemented stack. |
| **Further tools — only when useful** | Consider guided learning or optional personal features after the core experience works. Accounts, Supabase, and AI APIs are not prerequisites for the first version. |

This README establishes the foundation. It does not announce a completed course platform, live classes, or a certification program.

## Contributing

Readers, practitioners, teachers, designers, and developers can all help. A thoughtful question can be as useful as a code change.

Use an [issue][issues] to propose an improvement or discuss a larger idea before building it. For a focused change, submit a pull request that explains **what changed, why it helps, and how it was checked**. Contributions are subject to maintainer review.

For teaching material, include the relevant chapter and printed page, identify adaptations, and explain any safety or accessibility considerations. For scientific or health claims, provide appropriate primary sources and represent uncertainty honestly. Do not invent evidence, credentials, testimonials, or teaching outcomes.

Submit only material you have permission to share. Keep private journals, health information, access credentials, and sensitive location details out of public issues and commits. Obtain permission before contributing other people’s photographs or stories. Review AI-assisted work before submitting it and disclose material AI assistance in the pull request.

**Preserve the spirit of the school: clear teaching, careful attribution, respectful collaboration, and care for the living world.**

## Care and safety

This is an educational and contemplative project, not medical care. Yoga practices need to fit the individual; advanced poses are not requirements. Seek qualified instruction, and discuss relevant health conditions or pregnancy with your healthcare professional. Do not push through pain or force breathing. See [NCCIH’s yoga safety guidance][yoga-safety].

Choose suitable conditions and follow local access rules. Avoid unstable ground, hazardous branches, storms, and unsafe temperatures. Protect yourself appropriately from sun, insects, and irritating plants. Leave bark, roots, wildlife, and habitat undisturbed; do not assume a tree is a safe anchor for suspended equipment. Keep needed mobility aids and emergency or navigation tools available.

**Spiritual meaning and scientific evidence are distinct.** The original book includes interpretations about energy and health. New teaching should not turn those interpretations into medical guarantees or present electromagnetic protection or universal healing as established facts. Preserve the source honestly while reviewing practical guidance against appropriate evidence and safety standards.
