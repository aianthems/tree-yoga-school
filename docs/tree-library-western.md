# Western Library expansion and aspen companions — October 26, 2026

Five profiles bring the Library to 84 species: coast redwood (*Sequoia sempervirens*), Douglas-fir (*Pseudotsuga menziesii*), western redcedar (*Thuja plicata*), ponderosa pine (*Pinus ponderosa*), and Pacific madrone (*Arbutus menziesii*). Each uses the shared species and outdoor-practice templates with sourced identification, seasonal observation, three contemplative themes, four new practice steps, book connections, reflection, and licensed photographs.

Two companion comparisons bring the collection to eight guides:

- Quaking aspen and bigtooth aspen: mature leaf margins and winter bud scales. Fine versus coarse leaf teeth and hairless versus hairy bud scales are combined with notes about overlapping leaf shapes, image scale, and the limits of a photograph.
- Quaking aspen and eastern cottonwood: mature leaf outline and age-dependent bark. Leaf movement and flattened stalks are explicitly not exclusive to quaking aspen, and rough older aspen bark is not treated as a contradiction.

The comparison template supports two or more feature sections. The six earlier guides retain their three original sections and October 10 reference-review date. The two new guides show October 26. Source labels now identify the linked provider correctly, including Native Plant Trust references rather than labeling all links as NC State.

## Botanical review

NC State Extension profiles were retrieved October 26 for all five species. Oregon State University references supplement Douglas-fir, ponderosa pine, and Pacific madrone. Ponderosa pine uses Oregon State as its primary identification source: bundles of three or sometimes two are supported without collapsing separately classified regional pines into a universal needle-count rule. Broad leaves and scale leaves retain separate discovery categories; Pacific madrone is a broadleaf evergreen and western redcedar has scale-like foliage. Coast redwood, giant sequoia, and dawn redwood remain distinct profiles.

Aspen comparisons were checked against Native Plant Trust Go Botany profiles for *Populus tremuloides*, *P. grandidentata*, and *P. deltoides*, alongside the existing NC State sources. Comparative claims are educational starting points rather than complete identification keys. Images show individual examples at different scales and stages.

Douglas-fir joins the related-tree Pinaceae grouping along with the previously omitted true-fir genus *Abies*. Champion lookup uses the existing exact scientific-name policy without rewriting register records or silently merging synonyms. Aliases cover alternate common spellings such as Douglas fir, western red cedar, and Pacific madrona.

## Photography and integration

Seventeen new photographs were checked visually against source labels and licenses: fourteen for the five profiles and three supplementary comparison photographs. The close cottonwood leaf was selected over a more distant canopy view. Credits, original URLs, licenses, dimensions, and alt text are retained in `scripts/fixtures/tree-library-western-photo-sources.json`, visible captions, and `public/images/trees/ATTRIBUTION.md`. Originals were oriented, resized to at most 1200 pixels without upscaling, and converted to WebP. Share-alike licenses remain in force. No generated identification images or copyrighted Go Botany photographs were used.

Profiles enter the shared species array, search aliases, identification metadata, champion backlinks, related-tree suggestions, and generated routes. Comparison links appear in the directory and participating profiles. The sitemap has 370 URLs, including ten new species/practice URLs and two new comparison URLs. Current README totals are generated; historical notes remain dated snapshots. Practices and contemplative interpretations are new AI-assisted digital-school adaptations, reviewed against the linked botanical sources, rather than original-book quotations or medical claims.
